Constitutional Powers of the President

The president is the most powerful public official in the United States. The US Constitution is the basis of the president’s power. Article II says, “Executive Power shall be invested in a President . . .” Thus, the president’s main job is to execute or carry out the laws passed by Congress.

The Constitution provides the president with the following powers and responsibilities:

  • Appoint officials of the executive branch (usually with Senate approval)
  • Appoint federal judges (with Senate approval)
  • Serve as the ultimate commander of the armed forces (commander in chief)
  • Issue rules that the executive branch agencies must follow, known as executive orders
  • Veto laws passed by Congress
  • Pardon those convicted of federal crimes
  • Give several speeches each year to inform Congress on the “State of the Union”
  • Create a budget to be given to Congress for consideration

Along with powers listed comes unofficial power, such as the attention that comes from being president of the United States. A president can get the public’s attention and use this to present policy ideas. The president can impact foreign relations through personal meetings or policy speeches aimed at swaying international opinion. Because of the nature of the office, the president has flexibility to achieve political goals until he or she runs into Congressional opposition or violates the Constitution with presidential actions (usually executive orders).
