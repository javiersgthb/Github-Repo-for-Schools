Why Bureaucracy?

Bureaucracy has a bad name. People picture standing in line and filling out forms that will be given to expressionless office workers. The fact is that bureaucracy is actually the most efficient way to organize large groups of people. Even dynamic and profitable private companies have bureaucracies in place to manage the flow of work that needs to get done.

Think about your cell phone, for example. The Federal Aviation Administration (FAA) regulates the launch of the satellites that form the backbone of the internet. The FCC (Federal Communications Commission) regulates data transmission so signals do not overlap and scramble your data. The FCC also regulates the construction of your cell phone so that it can function on a network with hundreds of millions of other smartphones. Without those regulations, your phone is an expensive brick.

Hierarchical Authority

Bureaucracies are designed in a pyramid formation. A few officials at the top of the organization have authority over those officials and units at the larger, middle level, who in turn direct the activities of the many at the bottom. This allows for clear leadership. Imagine a workplace where all workers had equal authority—it’s easy to see how order would break down.

Job Specialization

Each bureaucrat, or person who works for the organization, has certain defined duties and responsibilities. Specialization allows workers to improve within a narrow set of specific tasks, which makes the overall workplace more efficient.

Formalized Rules

Work is done according to a set of established regulations and procedures. These allow all workers in a large organization to follow the same procedure. In large organizations, many different people might end up working with the same project or problem. Formalized rules make it easier to hand work off from one person to another.
