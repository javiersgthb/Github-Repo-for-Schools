Federal Agencies

All of the laws and programs created by Congress are useless without a mechanism to enforce and carry out the law. For example, in 1935, the Social Security Act was enacted by Congress to provide old age and survivor’s benefits to millions of Americans. The law entitled Americans to the benefits, but an organization was needed to collect data on Americans and make sure the benefits were paid out. The Social Security Administration exists today to carry out the goals set by the Social Security Act.

Dozens of federal bureaucracies exist to carry out the many jobs taken on by the federal government.

Staff agencies

Staff agencies are created to help advise executive officials (especially the president) on policy matters. Examples of staff agencies include the National Security Council (NSC) and the Office of Management and Budget (OMB).

Line agencies

Line agencies are agencies that run the programs for which they’re created. Examples include the Internal Revenue Service (IRS) or the Environmental Protection Agency (EPA).

Most of the people that work for federal agencies are career employees, which means they’re in their jobs based on merit and will serve in their roles regardless of who is the president. However, political appointees are at the very top of most agencies. These are people put in place by the president to fulfill their goals for the government. Think of the career employees as being the people with the know-how and the institutional memory to complete a job, while the political appointees are there to steer the bureaucracies in the direction desired by the president. There can be conflict between the leadership and career employees if their interests diverge. For example, a presidential directive for an agency to find ways to downsize the workforce might face some opposition from the career employees.

As time has gone by, the federal government has changed from a relatively small bureaucracy filled with political cronies to a very large series of organizations with mostly professional employees. No system is perfect, but this change to a more professional system is a benefit for the American people. The size of government itself, however, can be a debated issue.
