Key Points

  • The president of the United States serves as the head of the executive branch and is in charge of carrying out and enforcing laws.
  • The president is elected to four-year terms and has a two-term limit.
  • The president plays many roles while in office and has flexibility in the form of executive orders to control how federal agencies function.
  • The president isn’t elected through a straight popular vote, but through a process known as the electoral college.
  • The electoral college is criticized due to the way it counts some votes more than others and allows for the popular vote loser to still win the presidency.
  • The federal bureaucracy is made up of dozens of agencies and departments tasked with carrying out and enforcing the laws of the United States.
  • Bureaucracy is the most efficient way to organize large groups of people.
  • The federal bureaucracy has grown significantly throughout the history of the United States and has become a mostly professional operation.
