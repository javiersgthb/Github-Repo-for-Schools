The Declaration of Independence

Even after the battles of Lexington and Concord, the Second Continental Congress didn’t, at first, pursue independence. The Continental Congress sent several delegations to London to negotiate. However, Britain wasn’t going to yield under any circumstances. The Congress’s most important action was selecting George Washington as military commander. It was more of a political decision than a military one.

The Second Continental Congress selected George Washington to be military commander.

After armed clashes at Bunker Hill and elsewhere, King George III formally proclaimed the colonists in rebellion. Thomas Paine and other leaders promoted the Revolution as an opportunity for self-government by the people. Others wanted a government of political elite without a monarchy.

Thomas Paine’s Common Sense persuaded the American public to go “all in” and fight for independence and liberty.


On July 4, 1776, the Second Continental Congress approved the Declaration of Independence.

The most revolutionary aspects of the Declaration were its statements that

  • People have natural rights to life, liberty, and the pursuit of happiness.
  • Governments derive their power from the consent of the governed.
  •  People have a right to overthrow oppressive governments.

Jefferson’s Declaration of Independence deepened the meaning of the struggle by proclaiming its foundation in self-evident and universal human rights. Keep in mind that all the men who signed the Declaration of Independence literally had signed their own death warrant if they lost or were captured by the British.
