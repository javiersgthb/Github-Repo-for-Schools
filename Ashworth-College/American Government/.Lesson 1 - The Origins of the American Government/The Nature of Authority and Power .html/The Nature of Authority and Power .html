The Nature of Authority and Power

Power

Power refers to a person’s ability to control the world around them, including other people’s behavior.

Authority

Authority is the use of power seen as legitimate by society.

Politics and government are ways people in society achieve power. Politics is pursuing that power, and government is determining how that power is distributed and exercised.

Can a person have power without authority?

While power and authority influence the world around you, a person can have power without authority.

A crude example would be someone physically shoving another person out of a line to take their place. They have exerted control over those around them, but not in a way that most people would see as a legitimate or acceptable use of power.

However, if a police officer were called to the scene of the line-related tussle and arrested the person causing problems, most people would agree that the police officer’s use of force in this case would be acceptable. This action by the police officer would be an example of authority in action.

According to sociologists and political scientists, there are different varieties of both power and authority.

Types of Power

Expert

Power assigned to people based on their knowledge or skills

Coercive

Power to apply negative consequences on people who don’t follow your commands or ideals

Reward

Power to grant rewards (such as pay for work) or remove negative situations

Positional

Power that comes with the position a person holds in society or an organization, such as the manager of a store or the president

Referent

Power that comes from a group’s ability to convince others to follow a course of action

Like power, authority can also be classified in different ways. Sociologist Max Weber identified three types of authority: charismatic, traditional, and legal-rational.

Charismatic Authority

Charismatic authority refers to people who use their force of personality to gain referent power seen as legitimate by society. History is filled with leaders given power by their societies based on their charisma.

Traditional Authority

Traditional authority is seen in societies where those in power remain in power because the leadership reflects a continuation of an established ruling system. Such a system would maintain its authority through the preservation of tradition.

Legal-Rational Authority

Legal-rational authority is derived from people in power using their positions in society. The office itself holds the authority rather than specific office holders.


In the United States, all three types of authority can play a role in society. The American government has had charismatic leaders. Ronald Reagan used his popularity to push many of his legislative priorities. The Constitution provides legal-rational authority to the nation’s officeholders.

While you might not think of it this way, most societies grant traditional authority just based on the idea of doing things the way they’ve always been done. If a police officer asks you to leave an area, do you do so because of your knowledge of legal-rational authority, or do you comply because it’s tradition to listen to the police?
