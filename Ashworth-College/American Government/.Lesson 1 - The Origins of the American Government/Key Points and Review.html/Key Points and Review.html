Key Points

  • Politics is the process in which people draft, change, and preserve rules under which they agree to live.
  • Power refers to a person’s ability to control the world around them. In many ways, politics is driven by gaining power.
  • Authority refers to the use of power seen as legitimate by a society.
  • Government is how societies organize themselves to make the rules people have to follow, distribute resources, and resolve disputes.
  • All governments have three types of power:
      Legislative-the power to make laws
      Executive-the power to carry out and enforce laws
      Judicial-the power to interpret laws
  • The United States is a constitutional republic. This means that the government is limited to powers spelled out in its founding document, the Constitution, and the people have the power to select leaders who make rules on their behalf.
  • Much of American government is based on the ideas of Enlightenment thinkers—particularly Thomas Hobbs, John Locke, and Charles Montesquieu.
  • The cost of the French and Indian War and the British mercantilism led to an escalating set of taxes and resistance that resulted in the Revolutionary War.
  • After the Revolutionary War, the United States operated under a plan of government called the Articles of Confederation. This plan was flawed by a lack of central organization, causing leaders to call a constitutional convention.
