The American Revolution and the War of Independence

The American Revolution refers to a deep-seated transformation of thinking and loyalty that began when the first settlers arrived in America, and it finally led to the colonies’ political separation from Britain.

Fueling the American Revolution was the ongoing tension between the colonists’ newfound freedom and the oppression felt from ongoing participation in the British Empire’s mercantile system. Although British mercantilism provided economic benefits to colonists, its limits on freedom and patronizing goal of keeping America in a state of perpetual economic adolescence enabled growing resentment.

The American War of Independence was a military battle that began in 1775 and continued to 1783. The War of Independence began with British attempts to impose higher taxes and tighter imperial controls after the French and Indian War. To the British, these were reasonable measures under which the colonists would simply bear a fair share of the costs of the empire. To the colonists, however, the measures constituted attacks on fundamental rights. The British government felt that if it was providing protection to the colonies, the colonies should pay part of the bill.
