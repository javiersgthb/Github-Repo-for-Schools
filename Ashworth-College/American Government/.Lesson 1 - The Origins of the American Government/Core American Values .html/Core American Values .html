Core American Values

There are many ways the government of a nation reflects its people and values. No discussion of American government would be complete without examining the core values reflected in the US government.

Some of these core values include:

Select each item to learn more.

Individualism

Support for the value of the individual is enshrined in the Constitution through protection of property, rights, and personal privacy.

Personal Freedom

The Constitution was meant to be a blueprint for a government based on this value, allowing for as much freedom as possible. People should be free to live their lives as they see fit with minimal interference from the government. Generally speaking, a person’s freedom is limited when they infringe upon the freedoms of other people.

Majority Rule

Faith in majority rule, while respecting the rights of minority groups, is foundational to democratic ideals.

Compromise

The Founding Fathers expected lawmakers to work together to negotiate laws and government policy. Through compromise, it was hoped that wild swings in policy would be rare.

Equality

Note that equal outcomes (in wealth, success, and so on) aren’t guaranteed. However, it’s an American value to strive toward the government treating its people equally before the law.

The American government may not always live up to these values, but they’re reflected in the system of government, and American citizens pursue them as part of their political and cultural heritage.
