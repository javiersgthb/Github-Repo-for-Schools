Parts of the Constitution: Articles VI–VII

Article VI—Prior Debts, National Supremacy, Oaths of Office

Article VI could be considered a catch-all part of the Constitution. It made sure that all debts incurred before the adoption of the new government would be considered valid. This was important for establishing international trust and encouraging ongoing commerce. This article also included the following clause:

This Constitution, and the Laws of the United States which shall be made in Pursuance thereof; and all Treaties made, or which shall be made, under the Authority of the United States, shall be the supreme Law of the Land

This clause made clear that the Constitution was to be the supreme law of the nation under which all other laws must abide. It also had an impact on federalism as to when state laws conflict with federal laws. These conflicts are still settled on a case-by-case basis by the judicial branch.

Article VII— Ratification

Article VII set up the guidelines for the ratification (acceptance and adoption) of the new constitution. Early in the process, there was talk of requiring all 13 states to sign on for ratification, but that was lowered to nine states to make ratification more likely. This was either a brilliant or underhanded move depending on how you view the situation.

Which were the first and last states to ratify the Constitution?
