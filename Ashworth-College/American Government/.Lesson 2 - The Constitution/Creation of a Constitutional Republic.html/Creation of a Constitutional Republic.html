Creation of a Constitutional Republic

To appreciate what the framers of the Constitution sought to achieve, it’s important to understand the difference between a democracy and a republic.

Democracy

As the framers understood the word democracy, it referred to a political system in which the people decided public issues, either directly or through representatives, and without any curbs on their power.

Republic

In a republic, the power of the people was checked by a higher law (the Constitution), which prevented a majority from using the government to take advantage of a minority.

The framers supported the republican system because they feared a democracy would lead to tyranny of the majority. While this threat could never be completely eliminated, the framers believed it could be greatly diminished by properly structured representative institutions. They believed that political power should be responsive to the public, but that it also must be separated from direct control by voters to reach sound policies. For the framers, a republic meant a government made up of carefully designed institutions, defined as three separate branches in the first three Articles of Constitution.
