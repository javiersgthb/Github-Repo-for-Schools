The Three-Fifths Compromise

Another serious disagreement amongst the delegates from the states was how to count the enslaved population of the United States in terms of taxation and representation. States were going to receive representation in the House of Representatives based on population. This resulted in the following divide:

Southern States Northern States

Southern lawmakers wanted to count enslaved people for representation purposes. Since enslaved people couldn’t vote, this would give those who could vote a disproportionate amount of power in deciding the makeup of the future Congress.

Delegates from Northern states wanted to count enslaved people as people for taxation purposes, but didn’t want to count them as population for the House.


In the end, the convention came up with the Three-Fifths Compromise. This deal would count three out of every five enslaved people as the population for taxation and representation. In this way, slavery was shamefully included in the American Constitution.

This compromise would bring about agreement in the short term, but in the long term, this could be considered the root cause of the Civil War in the next century. It’s important to remember that some of the compromises presented during the Constitutional Convention were agreements of political expediency and relics of their time rather than results of sound political thought. Some of these agreements would later come to be viewed as exclusionary and antimajoritarian.
