Key Points

  • The United States’ original plan of government, the Articles of Confederation, provided for a central federal government too weak to address the needs of a new nation.
  • The Constitutional Convention was called in 1787 to address the weaknesses of the Articles by publicly amending them, but it would end up devising a whole new plan for the government in the Constitution.
  • During the Constitutional Convention, the Federalists favored a stronger national government, while the Anti-Federalists wanted to keep the bulk of the power with the states.
  • The Three-Fifths Compromise enabled slavery as a legal practice in the Constitution.
  • The Constitution set up the branches of government, the powers held by each branch, and the interaction between the states and federal government.
  • The amendment process in the Constitution ensures that changes to the constitution are rare and difficult to achieve without widespread political support.
  • The Bill of Rights was added to the original Constitution to ensure a measure of protection against an overreaching government.
