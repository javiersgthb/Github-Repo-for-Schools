Key Points

  • Political parties form on the basis of shared beliefs. They try to get their candidates elected into office to turn those beliefs into government policy.
  • Currently, the United States is in an era of divided government. No single party has had complete control of the federal government for long periods of time.
  • A party platform is a list of all stances a party takes on issues important to Americans. A plank is a party’s stance on an individual issue.
  • The United States has a two-party system. This is mostly because of its elections’ first-past-the-post voting, where the winners serve what are known as single-member districts.
  • A third party is a party that isn’t one of the two major political parties, which are the Republican and Democratic parties.
  • Interest groups are different from political parties in that they mainly care about influencing those in office, not necessarily getting certain people elected.
  • The skyrocketing costs of political campaigns in the past few decades have become a major concern to many political observers.
