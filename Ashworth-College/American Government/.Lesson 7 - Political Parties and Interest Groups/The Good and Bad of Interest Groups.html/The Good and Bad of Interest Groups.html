The Good and Bad of Interest Groups

Interest groups provide value to the government and citizens of the country. They can be thought of as a linkage mechanism connecting citizens with government. Like political parties, they use their resources to gain support for their ideas and inform voters. Although political parties are also linkage mechanisms, they serve a much broader range of issues in order to appeal to more constituents. Interest groups give citizens a way to get involved in politics in a more organized manner.

Interest Group Advantages 

  • Connect citizens with government
  • Use resources to gain support and inform voters
  • Provide specialist information to lawmakers
  • Donate resources and manpower to election campaigns

Interest Group Disadvantages 

  • Affecting politicians’ sound judgment with money
  • Spread of misinformation
  • Ambiguity in public support

Interest groups can also provide specialist information to lawmakers and often help in crafting legislation regarding areas of concern. Perhaps most importantly, interest groups are willing to put up the resources and efforts needed to get those that agree with them elected.

However, there are negative aspects of interest groups as well. Since they tend to provide much of the financing for political campaigns, there’s always the risk that campaign funding rather than sound judgement is swaying elected officials’ decisions. Interest groups can now directly spend money supporting their preferred candidates, and with this spending can come misinformation. It can also be difficult to determine exactly who makes up an interest group and how much popular support their ideas really have. With money and modern technology, a relatively small group of people can give the impression of wide-scale support for their ideas.
