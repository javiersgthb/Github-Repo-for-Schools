The Role of Lobbyists

Lobbyists are professional individuals who represent interest groups. Many are former legislators and lawmakers and a normally highly compensated for their efforts to influence legislation. Lobbyists can play significant roles, often helping to draft legislation and recommend strategies for getting bills passed into law.

Select each item to learn more.

Direct Lobbying

In direct lobbying, interest groups meet directly with an officeholder to get them to support or change policy.

Drafting Legislation

Lobbyists draft bills for congressmen to introduce to the House of Representatives or Senate.

Research

Lobbyists repair reports about legislation to attempt to sway opinion and votes as well as inform politicians about issues.

Hearings

Staff testify before the Congress to support or attack pieces of legislation.

Grassroots Lobbying

Interest group members aid lobbyists by contacting politicians, recruiting members, or protesting.

Electioneering

Interest group members raise funds to aid candidates.
