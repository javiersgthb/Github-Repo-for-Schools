Third Parties

Third party or minor party is a name given to a party that isn’t one of the two major parties. Examples include the Green Party and the Libertarian Party. While these parties rarely win major elections, they do have an impact on American politics.

The Spoiler Effect

The first impact is what’s known as the spoiler effect. There have been elections in which third parties win enough votes that, if their votes had gone to a major party candidate, the outcome of the election would have been different. A famous example of this is the 1992 presidential election. Independent candidate H. Ross Perot, an oil billionaire, received 19 million votes, 19% of the total, but won no states and netted zero electoral votes.

Some people believe that Perot, a conservative, played the role of spoiler by siphoning votes from fellow conservative George H.W. Bush, thereby handing the election to Bill Clinton.

Instigators of Change

Another important role third parties play is that of the critic and innovator. Third parties are often critical of actions taken by the major parties and are willing to take stances on new issues that the major parties may avoid. For example, H. Ross Perot made the national debt an election issue. The two major party candidates were forced to take stances on the debt as the issue became more popular.
