Money in Politics

In the 2020 election, about $7.9 billion were spent in running the various presidential and congressional election campaigns. Election campaigns have become increasingly expensive. The vast majority of candidates running for office don’t self-fund. In fact, neither of the major party candidates in 2020 self-funded. The money must come from somewhere, and that somewhere happens to be interest groups and wealthy individuals.

The question becomes, what if the candidates become corrupted by the money? Since it can be difficult to win without campaign funds, are future office holders beholden to looking favorably on the policies of those that supported their campaigns? While it looks like such donations might be borderline bribery, most political spending is protected by the current understanding of the First Amendment. In other words, political contributions are protected as free speech. In the Citizens United decision, the Supreme Court ruled that there should be few limits on campaign spending by interest groups.
