The Weakened Political Party?

Political observers have noticed in modern politics that the political parties don’t have as much power to compel loyal behavior from member lawmakers as they once did. Since the late 1800s, reforms have been put in place, such as the direct election of senators, that give voters more say in nominees and take that say away from party leaders.

Technology has also changed modern politics. Professional campaign managers employ advertising for their candidates on much larger scales than has ever been possible. Heavy use of television and social media is now the norm. All of these changes have made it easier for a candidate to run for office without the support of a party. Candidates can now “speak” directly to the electorate. President Trump was a perfect example of this in the 2016 election. Most Republican leaders didn’t want Trump as their candidate for the election, but he used the media to take his message directly to the voters.
