What Do Parties Do?

It can be easy to see the negative effects of party politics, such as the extreme partisanship and focus on winning elections instead of effective policy. However, there are important roles that the parties play.

Select each item to learn more.

Public Involvement

The parties provide a vehicle for average people to get involved. You could try to get a stance on a political issue out to the public by yourself, but your effectiveness would be limited. If you got together with a large group of people who shared your views, you could amplify your voice and achieve some goals. This is the basis of a political party—people coming together to achieve their political goals.

Structure

Parties also provide structure and leadership for political activities. In each of the 50 states, there’s a Democratic and Republican political party. They all differ in stances that affect their respective states.

Leadership

The parties take on the task of identifying and supporting qualified candidates for office and focusing on the issue they think most Americans would be interested in. Parties may select candidates based mostly on self-interest, but hopefully, competence increases the odds of election. When a president takes office, it’s the political party that will help the new president find loyal and hopefully capable people to fill roles in the executive branch.

Accountability

The parties love to act as watchdogs on each other. Pointing out wrongdoing or failings of the other party is self-serving but does keep the public informed as to possible government wrongdoing.

Get Out The Vote (GOVT)

Parties mobilize voters through grassroots efforts and campaign strategies as part of a movement to get voters to the polls. They engage in voter registration drives, door-to-door canvassing of neighborhoods, and phone banking as part of a broad strategy to identify and encourage their supporters to cast their ballots by election day.

Fundraising

Political parties raise funds to finance campaigns, advertising, signs and commercials, and other election-related expenses. They also provide logistical support like organizing campaign events and rallies, providing travel for the candidates and the press that follow them, and coordinating media appearances for their candidates.
