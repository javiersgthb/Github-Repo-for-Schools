The Legislature Fact Sheet

Senate

Members: 100—two from each state

Terms: Six years—staggered elections, one-third of Senate elected every two years

Age Requirement: At least 30 years old

Residency Requirement: Nine years

Leadership: President pro tempore (ceremonial) and majority leader

  • The Senate is the public policy generalist—it’s smaller in number, so it’s hard to specialize in policy areas and issues.
  • There are loose rules of debate, such as filibuster cloture.
  • Leadership selects committee assignments based on years of service and party politics.
  • In budget areas, the Senate is a court of appeals.
  • The Senate confirms all presidential appointments such as judges, ambassadors, cabinet posts, and department heads.
  • The only vote needed for budget and appointments is a simple majority vote (50 percent + 1).
  • Treaty confirmations and conviction in an impeachment trial require a two-thirds vote.

The House of Representatives

Members: 435, based on state population

Terms: Two years

Age Requirement: At least 25 years old

Residency Requirement: Seven years

Leadership: House Speaker, selected by the majority leaders

  • The House is the policy specialist—members spend 95 percent of their time on budget issues and very detailed work.
  • Party politics is the name of the game.
  • The House follows strict rules of debate.
  • The majority leader and minority leader pick committee chairs and assignments.

All members of Congress have generous perks and privileges.
