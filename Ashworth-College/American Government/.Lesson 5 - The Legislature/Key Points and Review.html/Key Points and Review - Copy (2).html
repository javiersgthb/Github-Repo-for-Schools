Key Points

  • The main job of the legislative branch is to make the laws. The process of passing bills into law is difficult and frequently slow.
  • Congress is made up of two houses—the House of Representatives and the Senate.
  • Members of the House serve two-year terms, and each state gets representatives in Congress proportional to its population.
  • Senators serve six-year terms and represent their entire state. Each state has two senators.
  • The highest-ranking person in the House is known as the Speaker of the House. In the Senate, the leader is known as the Majority Leader. In each case, the majority party picks the leader.
  • Since the Senate doesn’t have a rules committee that could limit debate, a tactic known as the filibuster is possible, where senators use overly long speeches to delay a vote on a bill.
  • Both the House and Senate form committees to break up the work of Congress and make Congressional responsibilities more manageable.
  • Lawmakers are interested in getting elected and staying elected. Because of this, they’re potentially pulled in different directions by their voters, their parties, and interest groups.
