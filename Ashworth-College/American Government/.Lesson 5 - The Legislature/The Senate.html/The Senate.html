The Senate

The leadership in the Senate belongs to whichever party controls the majority in the Senate.

The president pro tempore is an appointed person with ceremonial power in the Senate.

The majority leader holds the real power in the Senate—the power to set the agenda and control (for the most part) what bills get considered.

The majority leader also has the power to schedule hearings for the Senate approval of presidential appointments (including judges). This power was displayed in 2016, when majority leader Mitch McConnell refused to schedule hearings for President Obama’s appointment to the Supreme Court, Merrick Garland. 

The election of members of Congress is conducted by the individual states. Most candidates for Congress win nomination through a direct primary. Apportionment is the allocation of legislative seats in each state. The Supreme Court’s “one person, one vote” rule means that the populations of legislative districts must be effectively equal. Reapportionment typically occurs every ten years after each official US Census.
