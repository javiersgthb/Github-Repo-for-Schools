Federal Cases

As a consequence of the separation of state and federal governments in the US system, court cases can arise under either federal law or state law. More than 95 percent of the nation's legal cases are decided in state or local courts. Cases that originate in state courts can be appealed to a federal court only if a federal issue is involved, which usually isn’t the case. When a federal court becomes involved in a state case, it often confines itself to the federal aspects of the case, such as whether the defendant's constitutional rights were violated.

Cases that make it to the federal level of the judicial branch occur under two circumstances:

  1. They’re an appeal from a state supreme court into the federal system.

    or
  2. They fall under the guidelines of what would be a federal issue—federal laws and issues beyond the authority of individual states and cases appealed from the state supreme courts. This would include interstate crime, issues involving foreign citizens, and citizenship in general. It would also include issues involving the constitutionality of actions taken by the federal government. Most cases in this area start at the federal district court level, which serves as the court of original jurisdiction for federal cases.
