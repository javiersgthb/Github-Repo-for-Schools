Judicial Restraint and Judicial Activism

The doctrine of judicial restraint holds that the judiciary should be highly deferential to the judgment of legislatures. However, this rests on a presumption that elected lawmakers are acting constitutionally and should have broad authority to decide public policy. The duty of judges is to work within the confines of laws made by elected officials, seeking to discover their application to specific cases rather than searching for new ways to essentially change these laws.

Judicial Restraint→ Rarely overturns laws passed by the legislature

Judicial Activism→ Takes an active role in frequently overturning laws


Judges who want to take a hands-off approach and avoid overturning laws (leaving the lawmaking to legislatures) are said to be showing judicial restraint. Judicial restraint is based on the premise that when a court assumes policy functions that traditionally belong to elected institutions, the long-term effect is to uphold the fundamental premise of self-government—the right of the majority to choose society's policies.

Judicial activism is the idea that judges should take a broad view of judicial power and involve themselves as interpreters of the Constitution. According to this tradition, the courts should develop new principles when judges perceive a need to do so, particularly when it comes to individual rights. Judges who frequently overturn laws as unconstitutional are known as activist judges. The term “activist judge” gets thrown around quite a bit in political debates. Most of the time, it’s used by political partisans to describe decisions they disagree with.
