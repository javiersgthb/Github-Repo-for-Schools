Key Points

  • Precedent is the idea that judges tend to follow legal decisions that have been made in the past.
  • The Supreme Court makes decisions that set precedence for the largest area—the whole nation.
  • There are two types of legal conflict—civil and criminal. In civil cases, the courts are asked to settle a dispute between two parties. In a criminal case, a person is accused of a crime, and suit is brought against the individual by the government.
  • Jurisdiction refers to the authority to hear a case in court. Original jurisdiction is the power to conduct the entire trial. Appellate jurisdiction is the authority to hear appeals.
  • An appeals case is held to decide if justice was done correctly in the court of original jurisdiction, not to retry the entire case.
  • Judicial review is a power held by the courts to strike down actions taken by the executive branch and laws passed by Congress (or state legislatures) if those actions or laws conflict with the Constitution.
  • "Activist judges" are judges who frequently overturn laws. Judges who show judicial restraint avoid overturning laws.
  • The Supreme Court is limited in the number of cases it can hear in a year so that it can focus on important constitutional issues.
