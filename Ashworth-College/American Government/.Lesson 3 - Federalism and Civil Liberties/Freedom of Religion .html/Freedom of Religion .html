Freedom of Religion

In the case of freedom of religion, the government must be careful to allow people to worship as they please while not involving religion in government.

The First and Fourteenth Amendments set out two guarantees of religious freedom.

Establishment Clause

This clause prohibits the establishment of an official government religion.

Free Exercise Clause

This clause prohibits the government from interfering in the “free exercise” of religion.

This creates an interesting situation in the United States. How does the government allow for the free expression of religion without favoring religion at the same time?

In many ways, the government encourages religious belief. Consider the following examples:

  • Religious donations are tax-free (as is religion-owned property).
  • Chaplains are allowed in the military service.
  • Oaths of office are often taken using a religious text as part of the swearing-in ceremony (although not required).

On the other hand, as a result of court cases challenging government actions based on the establishment clause, the following changes have been made:

  • Prayer led by or encouraged by any school official isn't allowed in public schools.
  • Overtly religious imagery is carefully regulated in public government buildings.

Recent cases have shown that if religious imagery or prayers are allowed, then all beliefs must be allowed, which has tended to cause local and state governments who attempt to display religious material to prohibit all displays. Court cases throughout the years have clarified and continue to settle what exactly is included or not allowed according to the establishment clause.

Free exercise introduces its own set of issues. Since there are tax benefits that can come with being a religious organization, some organizations whose legitimacy might be questionable may claim to be religious in nature. Because the government can’t establish what is or isn’t a religion, it typically has no role in determining the sincerity of religious belief.

As you can see, the limit of how far religious freedom goes changes with the times and with changing views of what the establishment of and free exercise of religion entails. This can be frustrating for people learning about such freedoms for the first time, because it would seem like there’s no clear answers for what is or isn’t allowed. The beauty of the system is this flexibility to change with the times as society demands.
