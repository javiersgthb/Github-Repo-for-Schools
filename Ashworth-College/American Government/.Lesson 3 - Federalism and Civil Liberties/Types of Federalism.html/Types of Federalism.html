Types of Federalism

While federalism is the shared power between the federal government and state governments, there are different types of federalism that define those shared powers: dual federalism and cooperative federalism.

Select each item to learn more.

Dual Federalism

Dual federalism divides the power of the country between the federal government and all 50 state governments. For this reason, dual federalism is sometimes known as “layer cake” federalism.

Cooperative Federalism

Cooperative federalism involves shared responsibilities and powers across levels of government. More flexible than dual federalism, states and the national government work together on different programs, issues, or goals. Often, this involves transferring money where the federal government offers grants or aid tied to specific programs or projects. For this reason, cooperative federalism is sometimes known as “marble cake” federalism.
