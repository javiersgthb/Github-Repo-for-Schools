Civil Liberties versus Civil Rights

People often use the terms civil liberties and civil rights interchangeably. The meaning of the two terms sometimes blends because they both deal with the rights of citizens. However, there are important differences between the two.

  • Civil liberties are personal rights of the people that the government cannot deny. They are about personal freedoms and protections from government interference. Generally, these are broad rights, like the right to hold personal beliefs and express yourself freely.
  • Civil rights are the rights of equal treatment protected by legislation prohibiting discrimination against people based on race, color, religion, sex, national origin, physical abilities, family status, and other factors. Civil rights are the rights to participate equally in society, like going to school, getting a job, voting, shopping, buying a car or a home, and traveling from one place to another freely.
