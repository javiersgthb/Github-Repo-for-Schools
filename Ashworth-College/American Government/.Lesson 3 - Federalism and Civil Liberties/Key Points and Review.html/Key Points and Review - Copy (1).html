Key Points

  • Federalism is the organization of government between the federal government and the state governments. Each level has its own responsibilities and powers.
  • The federal (national) government typically has power over foreign affairs, military matters, and interstate commerce and conflicts.
  • The state level of government handles public order (police, emergency services), education and taxation, and commerce within state borders.
  • Powers overlap between the federal and state level of governments in terms of power of taxation, road building and maintenance, and the trying of criminals in court.
  • The federal government has increased in power since World War II through the use of intergovernmental revenue.
  • Civil liberties can be seen as protections against government overreach into peoples’ lives.
  • The process of incorporation refers to the use of the 14th Amendment to apply the rights in the Bill of Rights to the state level of government.
  • The understanding and limits of individual rights changes as time goes on and with a changing society. Exactly what’s meant by freedom of speech and freedom of religion has been reinterpreted by the American court system throughout the years.
