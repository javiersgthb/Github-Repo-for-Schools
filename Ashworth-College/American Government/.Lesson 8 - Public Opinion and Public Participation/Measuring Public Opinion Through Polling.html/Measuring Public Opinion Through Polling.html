Measuring Public Opinion Through Polling

Another major way of determining public opinion is simply asking people what they think.

There are two general types of polls: straw polls and scientific polls.

Select each item to learn more.

Straw Polling

In straw polls, any number of people may respond to the question asked. These polls are frequently seen online, and anyone can vote in them, usually a number of times. These polls should be seen as curiosities at best since they aren’t designed to give accurate results.

Scientific Polling

Scientific polls attempt to reflect the overall population being questioned as accurately as possible. These polls try to get an accurate sampling by specifically building a representative sample size or by selecting people for the poll randomly.

With scientific polling, the accuracy of the poll depends on the number of people polled. The more people polled, the lower the margin of error for that poll. The important thing to remember is that any single poll is a snapshot of public opinion at a specific time, with only a sample of the group of people being studied. Polls aren’t 100-percent accurate, but studying trends in polling can give an idea of what American citizens think about the government.

Accurate polling depends on several factors:

  • Constructing a representative sample
  • Neutral wording of the questions to be asked
  • Professional data-collection techniques
  • Openness regarding polling method

A word of caution—if a polling company refuses to provide its methodology, be wary of the results. Unfortunately, many polls exist for propaganda rather than informational purposes.
