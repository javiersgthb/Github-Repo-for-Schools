Popular Opinion Reflected in Policy

Public opinion certainly influences issues discussed during a political campaign season. A savvy campaign will attempt to get the attention of voters using issues that are popular.

An important question to consider is: Does that concern for public opinion continue once officials are in office?

Unfortunately, recent studies have shown the answer to be: not really. Legislators tend to support issues supported by moneyed interest groups, often at the price of what the public might want. Governmental stances on trade, taxation, and business regulation, in particular, tend to be at odds with public opinion. It would seem that money to support campaigns speaks louder than public opinion.

Lack of Knowledge Tribal Nature of Politics

Most Americans don’t pay attention to current events enough to understand the workings and developments of the government. As a result, they simply don’t know whether politicians are acting according to the public’s wishes or not. Come campaign time, large amounts of money (supplied by private interests) are spent convincing Americans that “promises have been kept.”

The tribal nature of modern politics also gets in the way of voters holding their elected officials to account. The actions of officials on individual issues have taken a back seat to partisan competition. Winning and losing have become more important than what party candidates actually do once in office.
