What is Government?

Government is how societies organize themselves to make the rules people have to follow, distribute resources, and resolve disputes.

All governments have three types of power:

Legislative

The power to make law

Executive

The power to enforce and carry out laws

Judicial

The power to interpret laws



In the United States, the three branches of government are named for these types of power according to their function. The Constitution lays out how these branches interact and outlines the basic processes and structure of government.

A government doesn’t need to resemble the government of the United States to have these powers. A king could hold all of these powers if that’s how his society is organized. Governments can take many different forms throughout the world. They can be classified based on where power is held in the governmental system.

Governments can be classified by the answer to the fundamental question: Who ultimately has the power?

Unitary System

In a unitary system, all power is held in a central national government.

Federal Government

In a federal government, power and authority are distributed between a national government and many state/local governments.

Dictatorship

A dictatorship is a government where one person has all the power.

Democracy

In a democracy, many people have the power.

In the United States, the government is a type of democracy called a constitutional republic. It’s constitutional because the Constitution of the United States sets up the structure of government. In a republic, many people have power to select the leader, who in turn makes policies on behalf of the people.
